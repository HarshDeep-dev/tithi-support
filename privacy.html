Privacy policy

Last updated 26 September 2026.

Tithi collects nothing about you unless you turn it on. There is no account to make, and the app works with every permission denied. Two things are optional and off until you switch them on: news from us, and limited usage data. Both are described below.

The short version. No accounts. No ads. No tracking, no advertising identifiers. Your tradition, your city, your location and your birth details never leave your phone. The calendar is computed on your device from data built into the app, so it works in airplane mode from the first launch. Usage data and news are shared only if you turn them on.

What the app stores, and where

Everything below is stored only on your own phone, in the app's private storage. It is never uploaded, and it is deleted when you uninstall the app.

WhatWhy
Your chosen traditionTo show that tradition's calendar
Your chosen cityTo compute sunrise, sunset and pachchakkhan times where you are
Your language, and display preferencesTo show the app the way you set it
Reminders you createTo schedule them on your device

Your tradition is a religious belief. Both app stores, the GDPR and India's DPDP Act treat that as a sensitive category, and the app is built so that it never leaves your device at all — which is the only guarantee worth making about it.

Location

The app asks for location only if you tap Use my current location, and never at launch. It asks for approximate location, only while the app is open. If you allow it, the app checks your approximate position when it opens, so that sunrise and the pachchakkhan times are right where you are. The position is used only while the app is running; it is never saved and never sent anywhere. The app never requests background location, and it works fully if you refuse: you can pick your city from a list instead.

Notifications

Reminders are scheduled by your phone, on your phone. No server is involved, nothing about a reminder is sent anywhere, and the app asks for notification permission the first time you open Reminders, or when you turn on reminders or Tithi updates — never at launch. Refusing it leaves every other part of the app working.

Tithi updates & announcements (optional, off until you turn it on in Reminders). Occasional news from us, such as new features or festival greetings, delivered by Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging. No push token exists until you turn it on. Turning it on gives your install a push token and subscribes it to one topic for the app's language (English, Hindi or Gujarati). Every phone on that topic gets the same message: we keep no list of devices and cannot send to you individually. Turning it off unsubscribes your phone and deletes the push token. Firebase also creates an installation ID on your phone the first time updates are turned on; it stays on your phone after you turn them off.

The network

The calendar, the texts and the tirth pages never use the network. The app holds the Android internet permission only for the two optional features above and below; with both off, it makes no requests of its own. (Google Play services may still keep its own connection on your phone, as it does for every app.)

Usage data (optional)

Off unless you turn on Share limited usage data on the app's Privacy screen. The app never asks for it during setup. When on, it sends Google Firebase Analytics which screens and features you open (for example, "opened the Kundali tool"), that the app started, that a reminder was created or a notification was opened, that a suggestion card on Today was shown or tapped, and the app's language. A screen is sent as its kind, never its contents: "a day page", not which day.

It never sends your tradition, sect or gaccha, your city or location, your birth details, or anything you type. It collects no advertising identifier, does not record which link, campaign or store page brought you to the app, and is not used for advertising, for tracking you across apps, or for building a profile of you. Google's own SDK also records basic facts such as the app version, the device model and a coarse region, and a random ID for your install. Turning the switch off stops collection at once and deletes that ID from your phone.

A future version may download corrected calendar data — if an advisor rules that a date should change, that correction needs a way to reach phones between store releases. When that ships, this policy will be updated before it does, and it is designed so the request still reveals nothing: the app downloads every changed data file regardless of which tradition you follow, and your tradition, city and location are never placed in a request.

What we never do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics unless you turn it on, as described above, and no crash-reporting SDK. Crash information, if any, comes only from Google Play and Apple's own developer consoles, in aggregate, and never identifies you.
  • No tracking across apps or websites. The app never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt, because it has nothing to ask for.
  • No selling of data, and no sharing beyond Google processing the two optional features for us.
  • No third-party content loaded at runtime. Even this page loads no fonts, scripts or images from anywhere else.

Children

Tithi is not directed at children under 13, and collects no data from anyone unless the optional features above are turned on.

Your rights

Everything the app stores is on your device and under your control: clear the app's data, or uninstall it, and it is gone. Turning off usage data deletes the install's analytics ID; the data already sent is linked to no name, account or contact detail, and Google keeps it for two months, after which it is deleted.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and any change that affects what leaves your device will be made before the app version that needs it is released.
