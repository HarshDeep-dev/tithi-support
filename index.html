Support

Tithi is a Jain panchang and a library of Jain texts. It works offline, it is free, and it has no ads.

Common questions

The dates differ from my local panchang

Each tradition names a day by its own convention, and two panchangs can disagree honestly — most often on a day where a tithi is doubled or lost. Check first that the tradition shown at the top of the screen is yours; you can change it in More. Every day page has a Why this date? panel explaining which rule produced the label. If you believe a date is wrong, please report it with the date and your tradition — see below.

Which traditions are included?

Five: Shwetambar Murtipujak Tapagaccha in both the one-tithi and two-tithi readings, Kharatara Gaccha, Sthanakvasi, and Digambar. Each is shown with its own conventions, and the tradition in use is always visible. A tradition is included only when its rules can be verified; where that is not yet possible, it is left out rather than guessed at.

Does it need the internet?

No. The calendar is computed on your phone and every text is built into the app. It works in airplane mode from the first launch.

Why won't it show my location?

The app asks for approximate location only when you tap Use my current location. If you refused, you can pick your city from the list instead — everything works the same way.

A reminder didn't arrive

Reminders are scheduled by your phone. Check that notifications are allowed for Tithi in your phone's settings, and that battery optimisation is not stopping the app. Reminders use inexact timing, so one may arrive a few minutes after the time you set.

Reporting a wrong date, or anything else

Corrections are genuinely welcome, particularly on dates. When reporting one, the most useful things to include are:

  • the date, as the app shows it, and the date you expected;
  • your tradition, and your city;
  • the panchang or source you are comparing against.

The app has a Report a discrepancy option on each day page, which prepares exactly this. Reports can also be raised at the public issue tracker.

The contact email for the app is listed on its Google Play and App Store pages.

About the data

The astronomical calculations use the JPL DE440s ephemeris, which is public-domain data from NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Calendar conventions per tradition are kept as separate, readable rules, and each carries a record of where it came from and who has reviewed it. Texts in the library carry their own source and licence, shown with the text.
